Foreground service demo

FOREGROUND_SERVICE_CONNECTED_DEVICE · continuous data transfer to an external device

What the video shows

BlueCard is a card game that people play together in the same room, each on their own phone. The phones are connected directly over Bluetooth; there is no internet server. One phone hosts the table and the other phones connect to it.

  1. Phone 1 opens a table. BlueCard starts a foreground service of type connectedDevice for the Bluetooth connection.
  2. Phone 2 finds the table and connects to Phone 1 over Bluetooth.
  3. The game starts; every move is sent between the phones over that connection.
  4. Phone 1 goes to the home screen. The ongoing notification “You are hosting a game” shows the service is running.
  5. Phone 2 plays a card. Phone 1, still in the background, receives the move and shows “It’s your turn!”.
  6. Tapping the notification returns to the game, which stayed connected and in sync.
  7. Phone 1 leaves the game: the service stops and its notification disappears.

Why a foreground service is needed

During a game the phones exchange moves continuously over Bluetooth. Players often switch apps, answer a message or turn the screen off while others take their turn. Without the foreground service Android would stop the app and drop the Bluetooth connection, which ends the game for everybody at the table. The service only runs while the user is at a game table and stops as soon as they leave it.
